
Q1	Descriptive analytics summarizes what happened last month, so it fits total orders, revenue and cancellation rate. Diagnostic analytics explains why delivery delays increased by 40% in Week 3 by examining operational factors. Predictive analytics fits forecasting expected order volumes for the next quarter using historical trends. The inputs are historical records for description, detailed operational factors for diagnosis, and historical/predictive variables for forecasting. The outputs are a status report, root-cause explanation, and forecast.

Q2	For current business health, Total Revenue = SUM(Revenue) and Cancellation Rate = Cancelled Orders / Total Orders × 100. Rising revenue generally indicates stronger sales, while a rising cancellation rate signals operational or customer-experience problems. For retention, Repeat Customer Rate = customers with 2+ orders / unique customers × 100 and cohort retention can be measured as retained customers / starting cohort × 100. Domain understanding matters because raw counts or averages can hide churn, cancellations, seasonality and customer-segment differences.

Q3	Mean delivery time is 38 minutes while median is 29 minutes, so the distribution is right-skewed: unusually long deliveries pull the mean upward. The 60 orders above 200 minutes contribute strongly to the mean and standard deviation. I would report the median as typical performance because it is less sensitive to extreme values. A standard deviation of 24 minutes indicates substantial variability. I would verify the GPS error first; confirmed measurement errors should be corrected or excluded, while genuine operational delays should be retained and explained.

Q4	I would use an independent two-sample t-test if the before and after orders are independent and the assumptions are reasonably satisfied. H0: mean AOV before = mean AOV after. H1: mean AOV after > mean AOV before. With p = 0.03 and alpha = 0.05, reject H0. In business language, the campaign produced a statistically significant increase in average order value at the 95% confidence level, although the company should also consider the size and cost of the effect.

Q5	VLOOKUP is limited because the lookup key must be in the leftmost column and the return column is normally selected by a column number. XLOOKUP can independently specify lookup and return arrays and has a not-found argument. INDEX-MATCH also separates the lookup and return ranges. Example: =XLOOKUP(C2,Restaurants!$A$2:$A$21,Restaurants!$B$2:$B$21,"Not Listed"). C2 is RestaurantID, the first range contains IDs, the second contains names, and the last argument handles missing IDs.

Q6	Use a Pivot Table with Cuisine in Rows and Revenue in Values, optionally putting Zone in Columns. Use a Pivot Chart for visual comparison. For weekly order volume, group or derive week from OrderDate and chart order counts. For top five zones by average rating, place Zone in Rows, Rating in Values summarized by Average, sort descending and apply a Top 5 filter. Slicers provide clickable field filters and Timelines provide date-range filtering. A formal Excel Table expands with appended data, making refreshes more reliable; without it, new rows may be excluded from the source range.
